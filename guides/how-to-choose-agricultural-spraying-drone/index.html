Agricultural drone buyer’s guide

How to Choose an Agricultural Spraying Drone

Updated October 9, 2026. Compare tank capacity, spray system, battery workflow, field fit, maintenance and support before selecting a 5–30 L platform.

Quick answer: Define the application first, then compare the complete operating workflow.

01

Define the agricultural mission first.

Begin with the material and the field task. Liquid crop-protection spraying, foliar feeding and dry-material spreading require different payload systems. A spraying configuration normally involves a tank, pump, hoses, nozzles and flow control. A spreading configuration instead requires a hopper, metering mechanism and distribution system.

Before requesting a quotation, record the crop, field layout, liquid or granular application, label-required application rate, typical plot size, refill process, available charging power and local operating requirements.

02

Select capacity around the complete workflow.

A larger tank can reduce refill frequency, but capacity alone does not determine daily productivity. The practical result also depends on application rate, usable payload, route length, terrain, refill location, battery turnaround and cleaning time.

Use capacity as a starting point, then confirm the exact aircraft and payload configuration against the intended application and operating conditions.

5–10 L
Compact transport, targeted work and smaller or fragmented fields.
16–21 L
A balance of useful capacity, transport, refill frequency and field flexibility.
30 L
Higher-capacity work supported by suitable batteries, charging, transport and refill equipment.

03

Evaluate the complete spray system.

Ask the supplier to document the approved pump, adjustable flow range, nozzle options, hose and filter arrangement, tank installation, flow calibration, spray shut-off and cleaning access.

Application quality is affected by more than advertised spray width. Nozzle selection, droplet size, height above the canopy, speed, wind and swath overlap all influence coverage and drift. Operators should follow the product label and local application requirements rather than rely on one universal setting.

04

Plan the battery and charging workflow.

Compare the complete energy system, not only the stated flight time. Published endurance can change with payload, battery condition, temperature, wind and flight profile.

Confirm the approved battery configuration, the number of batteries and chargers required, cooling and charging procedures, available electrical power, cycle management and the contingency plan if a battery or charger is unavailable.

05

Match the airframe to terrain and transport.

Compare folded dimensions, airframe layout, take-off area, transport vehicle and access to the fields. Operations across separated plots may give greater weight to folding, setup time and ease of loading.

Do not infer obstacle avoidance, terrain following, RTK performance or autonomous functions from the appearance of the aircraft. Request the exact controller, sensors and supported functions for the quoted configuration.

06

Check serviceability, training and support.

Confirm which routine items can be inspected or replaced locally, including propellers, motors, pumps, hoses, filters, seals, nozzles, connectors, batteries and chargers.

The delivery scope should state the recommended inspection schedule, spare-parts list, documentation, setup, calibration, ground testing, supervised flight testing and troubleshooting support that are included.

07

Review regulations before operation.

Drone and pesticide-application requirements vary by country and region. Buyers should check aircraft registration, pilot qualifications, operational approvals, pesticide labels, permitted application methods and local environmental or worker-safety rules.

For example, the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration states that 14 CFR Part 137 governs aircraft operations that dispense or spray certain substances for agricultural purposes. This is one national example, not a rule for every market.

Frequently asked questions

Is a larger spray tank always better?

No. A larger tank can reduce refill frequency, but the aircraft must still fit the field, transport, battery, charging, application-rate and maintenance workflow. Compare the complete operation instead of capacity alone.

What information should I send when asking for a quotation?

Provide the destination country, crop, terrain, spraying or spreading requirement, preferred capacity, application rate, required functions, purchase quantity and expected delivery schedule.

Can one agricultural drone support both spraying and spreading?

Some platforms may support approved spraying and spreading configurations, but the payload systems and operating setup are different. Confirm compatibility and the exact included equipment before ordering.

How should I compare published flight time?

Check the payload, battery, weather and test conditions behind the published value. Actual endurance changes with configuration and operating conditions, so flight time should be evaluated together with battery turnaround and refill time.

Do agricultural spraying drones require special approvals?

Requirements depend on the country, aircraft weight, substance and operation. Confirm aviation, pesticide-application, product-label and local safety requirements before use.
