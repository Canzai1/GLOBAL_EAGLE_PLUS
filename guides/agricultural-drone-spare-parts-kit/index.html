Agricultural Drone Spare Parts Kit: Compatibility and Support Checklist

Updated October 9, 2026

An agricultural drone spare parts kit should be matched to the aircraft model and its supplied configuration. Tank capacity or a product photograph is not enough to identify replacement parts, confirm compatibility or decide who should carry out a repair.

This checklist helps buyers, distributors and fleet teams prepare a parts discussion before ordering a complete drone or requesting replacements. It separates the parts list from the tools, documentation and service arrangements needed to support the aircraft.

Quick answer: Request an itemized, model-specific parts list. Confirm part numbers, revisions, quantities, compatibility, permitted service responsibilities and replenishment terms. Treat price, stock availability, warranty coverage and delivery time as separate items requiring written confirmation.

01

Identify the aircraft before selecting parts

Start with the exact aircraft model, available serial or identification details, and the configuration originally supplied. State whether the application system is for liquid spraying, granular spreading or another approved configuration.

Where available, include the relevant parts-diagram reference and existing component label. A controller, pump or spreading-system revision can change the parts required even when the aircraft name remains the same. Ask the supplier to check the identifiers rather than treating a visually similar item as an interchangeable replacement.

For a new aircraft purchase, request the parts list alongside configuration confirmation. For an existing aircraft, describe any approved changes made since delivery. Do not assume that a parts list for another brand or another model applies to your platform.

02

Organize the kit by purpose

A useful kit distinguishes routine service items from replacements that require diagnosis or specialist work. The following categories are questions to review, not a standard TYI package or instructions to repair an aircraft.

Liquid-system service itemsApproved filters, seals, tubing or nozzle components, with exact positions and part identifiers
Pump and flow-related componentsCompatible assembly and revision, diagnostic requirements and any checks needed after replacement
Propulsion componentsModel-approved propeller, motor or controller identifiers, installation responsibility and required verification
Airframe and landing structureExact structural assembly, damage-assessment requirements and repair authorization
Wiring, connectors and electrical modulesApproved component identity, electrical compatibility and who may service the assembly
Granular spreading components, if fittedSpreader version, compatible module parts and separate documentation
Battery and charging equipmentApproved equipment model, required information and the appropriate support route
Tools, documents and supportApplicable manual, authorized service scope, required tools and available technical assistance

Avoid ordering every category by default. The appropriate scope depends on the selected aircraft, service arrangements and expected use. Batteries and chargers should be discussed as identifiable equipment, not treated as universally compatible accessories.

03

Confirm compatibility in writing

Ask for the approved part number, revision and any applicable assembly position. Where a supplier proposes a substitute, request explicit confirmation of its suitability for the specific aircraft configuration and of any resulting documentation or support changes.

An item that fits physically may still differ in control interface, electrical requirements, material suitability or software support. Compatibility should come from the responsible supplier or technical team, not from appearance alone.

Keep that confirmation with the aircraft record. This makes a later replenishment request more precise and helps distinguish an approved revision from an undocumented substitution.

04

Separate parts supply from repair responsibility

A parts quotation should state what is being supplied. It should also clarify which tasks can be performed by trained operators, which require qualified service personnel, and which must be referred to the supplier or an approved service provider.

Request the applicable documentation, inspection requirements and release responsibilities. Do not assume that purchasing a motor, structural part or electrical module authorizes field replacement. A collision, suspected structural damage or electrical fault requires assessment under the platform's approved support process before operation resumes.

This guide does not provide fastening settings, calibration values, chemical-cleaning methods or return-to-flight instructions. Those must come from current model-specific documentation and qualified support. A spare-parts kit is not a substitute for a service plan.

05

Choose quantities using your own support plan

There is no universal number of spare parts per drone. Discuss the number of aircraft, intended workload, access to technical service, storage arrangements and the verified replenishment process.

For a new fleet, use the supplier's approved parts recommendation as a starting point and review it after actual service records are available. Do not invent replacement rates from a brochure or assume that every component should be stocked in the same quantity.

Maintain a simple record of part identity, aircraft compatibility, quantity on hand, date received and quantity used. Agree how damaged, unsuitable or superseded items will be kept separate from usable inventory. Follow the applicable equipment documentation for storage and handling.

06

Confirm replenishment and commercial terms

Separate parts availability from order processing and transport. Ask whether the requested item is available, subject to production, or requires further compatibility checks. Request the expected dispatch conditions and delivery basis for your destination.

The quotation should identify currency, quantity, packing, freight scope and any excluded charges. Where a replacement is being considered under warranty, obtain a written decision on eligibility and the claim process; do not assume all replacement parts are covered.

For batteries, charging equipment or contaminated components, request the appropriate handling and transport information from the supplier and carrier before arranging shipment. This checklist does not provide a shipping authorization or universal transport rule.

Avoid replacing these confirmations with a promise of worldwide stock, same-day dispatch or guaranteed operating uptime. The important purchasing outcome is a clear, traceable scope for the requested parts and support.

Prepare a parts-request brief

Send enough information for the technical team to identify the request without guessing. Use the following fields where available:

  • Aircraft model and identification reference.
  • Original configuration and relevant approved revisions.
  • Spraying or spreading system version.
  • Existing part label or parts-diagram reference.
  • A clear description of the request and relevant photographs.
  • Required quantity and whether this is planned stocking or a replacement request.
  • Destination country and preferred contact method.
  • Requested documentation, support and delivery-scope confirmation.

Share only information needed for the review. If photographs or logs contain customer identities, credentials or unrelated location details, remove those details or agree a suitable support channel first.

For a new complete-drone order, ask the supplier to show optional spares separately from the main aircraft package. For an existing aircraft, ask for parts identification and availability before treating an estimated cost as a final order.

Frequently asked questions

What should an agricultural drone spare parts kit include?

It should contain the agreed items for the exact aircraft configuration, with identifiable part numbers, quantities and service responsibilities. Liquid-system items, approved replacement components and supporting tools or documents are categories to review, not a universal included list.

Are parts interchangeable between drones with the same tank capacity?

Not necessarily. Capacity does not identify the airframe, propulsion, control electronics or application-system revision. Confirm compatibility with the supplier using the model and part identifiers before ordering.

How many spare parts should a fleet hold?

There is no fixed quantity suitable for every fleet. Base the discussion on aircraft count, workload, service access, actual maintenance records and the confirmed replenishment process. Avoid assuming a replacement rate before it is supported by evidence.

Can a spare-parts kit be used to repair a drone after a collision?

Having replacement parts does not establish that the aircraft is safe to repair or operate. Follow the approved damage-assessment and service process, and have the appropriate qualified personnel determine the required work and release checks.

Are spare parts and repair services included in the aircraft warranty?

That depends on the written terms and the specific claim. Confirm coverage, exclusions, the review process and any shipping or labor responsibility. This guide does not state a standard warranty period or promise replacement approval.

Yes. Complete agricultural drone supply remains TYI's primary offering. This guide helps clarify support and parts questions; it does not replace product selection, complete-aircraft quotations or the separate production-line service.
